The short version

Build one shared piece first: a one-column form for any diagram that is not a route. Then loosen two rules and allow five lanes. Then build the flowchart and the sequence diagram as new kinds. The commit graph waits.

Sequence diagram

Participants stand at the top, each with a track. Messages run from top to bottom. A reply is a thin muted line with an open arrowhead, because a dashed line already means "only some readers take it". A call to itself is a small loop. One group of alternatives is a set of bands, one band for each case.

On a phone the same data becomes a script: one row for each message, with its number, who sends it to whom, and the verb. The group stays a band.

Flowchart

The main path runs down one column. A decision is a plate with pointed ends. Its first exit goes on down; the other exits go right, to a second column. A stop has a thick outline. The arrow back rises between the columns and never crosses the main path.

On a phone, and for any other data that is not a route, the drawing becomes one column. A side exit is a short line under its decision. A ring with a number says which step a path goes back to.

Side legs beside walkthrough steps

The walkthrough keeps its route, so step n still lights leg n. One side leg goes from a part of the route to an extra part. It is dashed, it has no number, and no step lights it. This drawing is the renderer's real Route tall drawing, with the side leg added by hand.

Layers with a skipping arrow

A stack of layers is already possible: one lane, one part for each layer, a leg from each layer to the next, and one more leg that skips. Today it is Plates, 540px wide, and it scrolls on a phone. The one-column form gives it a narrow form: the skipping leg becomes a go-to line.

The rest, in words

Five lanes

With only the limit changed, a five-lane route on the current renderer is 922px wide as Route wide, 1022px as Route tall and 1100px as Plates. All three keep the spacing rules and fit a full-width well of 1104px.

Two chains in one figure

One drawing numbers its legs in one order, so two unrelated chains in it show a false order. A figure may hold two drawings instead. They stand side by side when both fit, and one below the other when they do not.

Looser rules

A leg may have no verb; it keeps its number. A long note no longer forces Plates: the renderer tries the route forms, and the spacing rules decide.

The commit graph

Declined for now. It has its own look and rules, and one document needed it. Two small drawings in one figure can show a merge and a squash today.
