Incident review: sync errors on 12 November

For 47 minutes on 12 November 2026, the Sync API failed up to 61% of requests, and people could not save changes in Example Planner. A configuration change had lowered the database connection limit from 200 to 20. No data was lost. This review gives the timeline, the causes, and seven actions.

  • 12 November 2026, 08:40 to 09:27 UTC
  • Review meeting 17 November 2026

At 08:31 a change to the shared configuration template reached production. It set the Sync API's database connection pool to 20, where it had been 200. Under the morning load in Europe, requests waited for a free connection and timed out.

The on-call engineer first treated it as database overload. The cause was found at 09:14 in the API logs, and the change was rolled back at 09:19. Errors were under 1% at 09:24.

Times All times are UTC. The morning peak in Europe starts at about 08:00 UTC.

Impact

Figure 1 shows the error rate of five parts of the service, in 5-minute windows. The Sync API and the web app's saves failed from 08:40 to 09:20. Reminders were slow, because they read through the same pool. Search did not use the pool and was not affected.

Figure 2 shows the cause plainly. The pool limit fell to 20 at 08:35, and the connections in use stayed pinned at the limit until the rollback. Just after the rollback, the waiting requests took 118 connections at once.

How the response went

Follow the dot. Each step names one arrow in the figure.

  1. The alert pages the engineer.

    The alert fired at 08:44, four minutes after errors passed 5%. The engineer acknowledged at 08:49.

  2. The engineer reads the logs.

    The first guess was database overload, and two replicas were added at 09:06. They did not help. The logs held the real cause from 08:35.

  3. The logs show the cause.

    At 09:14 a database engineer who joined the call saw "pool exhausted, limit 20", and found the change in the deploy history.

  4. The engineer rolls back.

    The rollback started at 09:19 and reached all regions by 09:24. No restart was needed.

What went well

  • The app kept every unsaved change and sent it again after recovery, so no work was lost.
  • The status page was updated 18 minutes after the first errors.
  • The rollback took five minutes and needed no restart.

What went badly

  • The change reached all regions at once, so no region stayed healthy.
  • No alert watches the pool, so the first guess was wrong and cost 8 minutes.
  • The runbook for sync errors does not mention the connection pool.

Why it happened

The direct cause was the pool limit of 20. Figure 4 groups the causes that let the change reach production and kept the outage long. No single person caused it; each cause is a gap in a process.

The change that caused it 
# Before: sync-api.yaml, as the template produced it
database:
  pool_size: 200
  timeout_ms: 2000

# After: the value moved to a new section, and the old key was ignored
database:
  timeout_ms: 2000
  pool:
    size: 20        # the new section's default
