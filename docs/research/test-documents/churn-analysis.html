Why customers left in the third quarter

In the third quarter of 2026, 1,212 paying accounts cancelled Example Planner, up from 812 in the second quarter. Almost all of the rise came from small Starter accounts after the price change on 1 July. This report shows where the losses came from and proposes three changes.

Starter accounts with 1 to 5 seats drove the rise. Their quarterly churn went from 13.0% to 21.4%. Team and Business accounts stayed close to their usual rates.

Two changes on 1 July explain most of it. The Starter price went from $8 to $12 for each seat, and calendar sync moved from Starter to Team. In the exit survey, 54% of the answers named one of these two changes.

Accounts that left also set up less. Only 31% of them connected a calendar in their first month, against 68% of the accounts that stayed.

Churn In this report, churn means paying accounts that cancelled. A paused account is not counted. See Method.

The trend by month

Monthly churn on the Starter plan stayed between 3.0% and 3.5% from October to April. It started to rise in May, when the price change was announced, and reached 6.4% in September. Team and Business did not move in the same way.

Why they say they left

Of the 1,212 accounts that cancelled, 734 answered the one question in the cancel form: "What is the main reason you are leaving?" Each account gave one answer.

Price was the most common answer. The second was the move of calendar sync to the Team plan. Together they make 54% of the answers. "Other" holds the free text that fit no group; the most common words there were "invoice" and "too many tools".

Who answered 61% of the accounts that cancelled. Accounts that cancel through their bank do not see the form, so they are missing.

How far accounts got with setup

We followed every Starter account that started to pay between 1 April and 30 June through its first month, and split them by whether they were still paying on 30 September. The accounts that left lost most at one step: connecting a calendar.

Of the accounts that left, 31% connected a calendar. Of the accounts that stayed, 68% did. This does not prove that a calendar keeps an account. Accounts that plan to stay may simply set up more. But it is the one step where the two groups differ most.

Risk

Change 1 brings back part of what the price change took away. If many Team accounts move down to Starter, revenue falls. Finance will watch moves from Team to Starter every week for the first month.

Method and limits

Method

We counted every paying account that cancelled from 1 July to 30 September 2026, by its plan and its seats on 1 July, and compared it with the second quarter. The main reason for leaving comes from the cancel form, and the setup steps from each account's product events in its first month.

Churn rate
Paying accounts that cancelled in a period, divided by paying accounts on the first day of the period.
Monthly revenue lost
The monthly price of every account that cancelled, before discounts, in US dollars.
Cohort
The accounts that started to pay in the same calendar month.
Paused account
An account that stopped paying for up to 3 months and kept its data. It is not counted as churn.

What was left out

Paused accounts are not counted as churn: 96 accounts paused in Q3. Free trials that never paid are not in the data. 478 of the 1,212 accounts that cancelled did not answer the cancel form, so the reasons in Figure 4 come from 734 answers.

How sure the result is

The rates count every account, not a sample, so they have no interval. The reasons are less sure: the accounts that answered the form may differ from the 478 that did not. The Business rate rests on 43 cancellations; without the one customer that moved 12 accounts to a single contract, it is 6.6%.

Why do the plan rates not add up to 14.4%?

Each plan's rate uses the accounts of that plan as its base. The rate for all plans uses all 8,420 accounts. Starter has the most accounts and the highest rate, so it pulls the total up. 18.2% of 5,310, plus 7.6% of 2,640, plus 9.1% of 470, is 1,212 accounts, or 14.4% of 8,420.
