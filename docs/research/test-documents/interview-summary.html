What eight teams told us about planning their week

In September we interviewed eight teams that use, or used, Example Planner. We asked how they plan a week, where the product helps, and where it gets in the way. This summary gives the six themes they raised, what they said in their own words, and a short survey of 167 customers that tests the themes.

Research summary
  • 10 minutes to read
  • Interviews 14 to 25 September 2026
  • Survey 29 September to 6 October 2026
  • Owner: Product research
  • Every person, quote and number is invented

What we found

  1. Two calendars are the main pain. Seven of eight teams keep a work calendar beside Example Planner, and double bookings follow.
  2. The seat price hurts small teams with part-time members. Six teams pay full seats for people who plan one day a week.
  3. Setup falls on one person. Five teams said one person spent an afternoon or more to set up projects and invite the team.
  4. The weekly review is the reason to stay. The teams that stay use the Monday review every week.

The survey agrees with the interviews on price and on the weekly review. It agrees less on setup: most survey answers found setup easy enough.

Read with care Eight interviews show what matters to some teams. They do not show how many teams feel the same. The survey gives a first count.

Two calendars clash

Seven teams keep their meetings in a work calendar and their plans in Example Planner. They copy meetings by hand, or they do not, and then a plan collides with a meeting. Three of the Starter teams lost calendar sync on 1 July.

7 of 8 All but P6. P6 uses no work calendar at all.

The seat price for part-time members

Small teams often have members who plan one or two days a week: a bookkeeper, a part-time designer, a volunteer. Each needs a full seat. Teams said they removed these people to save money, and then the plan was no longer complete.

6 of 8 Every team with a part-time member raised it.

Setup takes an afternoon

One person sets up the projects, the team and the first week. Five participants said it took an afternoon or more. The two teams that left both said the setup never felt finished.

5 of 8 The survey found setup easier than this. See Survey.

The weekly review is the main use

The teams that stay open the Monday review with the whole team. It is the one screen they all name. Three of them said they would not pay for the product without it.

5 of 8 None of the teams that left used the review every week.

Two smaller themes

  • A read-only seat for clients (4 of 8). Agencies and studios want clients to see the plan without a paid seat.
  • The phone app is for checking only (3 of 8). People look at the day on the phone, and plan on a computer.

What we suggest next

  • Test calendar sync for one calendar on the Starter plan. This links to the churn report of 9 October.
  • Design a lower price for members who plan one day a week, and test it with five of the interview teams.
  • Run five more interviews with teams that left in October, to check whether setup matters more for them.
Open question

Is a read-only seat for clients a reason to choose a plan, or only a wish? Four teams asked for it, but none said they would leave without it.

How we did it

  • Eight interviews of 45 minutes, by video call, each with one researcher and one note taker.
  • Participants chose a time from an email to 60 customers and 20 former customers. Each got a $50 gift card.
  • Two researchers coded the notes with the same theme list, and agreed on 44 of 48 codes before talking. They settled the other four together.
  • The survey went out by email from the product, with one reminder after three days.
The interview questions
  1. Walk me through how your team planned last week.
  2. Where does Example Planner help with that? Where does it get in the way?
  3. Think back to your first two weeks. How did each part feel?
  4. What would make you pay more? What would make you leave?
  5. Is there anything we did not ask that we should have?
